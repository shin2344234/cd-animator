# How the game's animation data maps onto Blender

Worked out on 2 October 2026 against build 2.03.02, using Kliff's skeleton (`phm_01.pab`, 447 bones), the broom's (`cd_r0032_00_broom.pab`, 9 bones) and the 44 broom riding clips, full and LOD. `tests/check_import.py` re-checks everything below except the axis handedness, which is a judgement from the bind pose.

The file formats themselves come from Broomy's research, its notes on clips and metabins and its `paa.py` for skeletons. The codecs in `cd_animator/formats` started as copies of Broomy's. Since 0.7.0 the clip codec follows the header flags and reads scale keys, and the skeleton reader no longer guesses the gap between bones, so Broomy's copies are older and read about a quarter of the game's clips. "Every layout the game ships" below has the details.

## A key sits on top of the bind pose

A bone's local transform in a clip is `bind @ key`, for rotation and position both. The position key is in the bone's bind frame, so it is turned by the bind rotation before it is added to the bind position.

The evidence:

- Read as absolute transforms the keys make no sense. `Bip01 Spine` has a bind offset of 7.8 cm from the pelvis and a position key of 0, and the thighs, whose bind turns them about 180 degrees, have rotation keys within 30 degrees of identity.
- Kliff's `Bip01` has a bind rotation of 90 degrees about Y. In the run clip its position key moves 6.80 m along +X while `B_MoveControl_01`, whose bind is identity, moves 6.86 m along -Z. Turned by the bind rotation the two are the same motion.
- The broom's idle pitches its `Bip01` 17.6 degrees about X. Kliff's idle turns his `Bip01` 16 degrees about his local Z, which his bind rotation makes the world X, so he tilts with the broom.
- Under this reading Kliff's idle frame is a seated rider, hands on the shaft, and his left and right limbs mirror each other. The other readings put his feet or head in the wrong place or break the symmetry.

Blender poses bones the same way (`rest @ basis`), so a key becomes a pose basis directly. The only extra step is the bone direction correction described in `convert.py`.

## The game is left-handed, Y up

In the bind pose Kliff's toes point to -Z and his left side is +X. Facing -Z with the left side at +X only works in a left-handed frame. Swapping Y and Z gives Blender's right-handed Z-up frame with Kliff facing -Y, left side at +X, which is how Blender characters normally stand. The swap is exact for both vectors and quaternions.

## Keys are normalised and interpolate the short way

Stored rotation keys are not unit length: half floats are off by up to 0.005, the 8-bit LOD keys by up to 0.05. Blender normalises pose quaternions, and the reference in the test does too.

Kliff's `Bip_Weapon_C` changes quaternion sign between neighbouring frames 20 times across the mount and dismount clips. Taken literally that is a 340 degree spin in one frame where the short way is 5 to 18 degrees, so the game must interpolate the short way. The importer flips signs so each track is continuous, which gives Blender the same result. An exporter can write either sign, since q and -q are the same rotation.

Between keys the importer uses linear interpolation (Blender normalises, so rotations come out as nlerp). That matches Broomy's LOD builder but has not been checked against the engine.

## The first transform record places a rider on its mount

Each rider clip's lead holds a transform record, a scale, rotation and position. Putting the rider's origin at the mount's origin moved by its rotation and position lines the two up. In both mount clips Kliff then starts with his toes exactly on the ground beside the broom (0.862 m to the left for `mount_l`, turned 90 degrees) and finishes exactly where the idle seats him. The idle's record is the seat, about 1.35 m up. "Seat rider on mount" in the add-on does this and follows whichever clip is active.

The second record, `ref_scale` and the four `B_TL_Position` bones are not used for display. Earlier versions split the two records at a different place and called the parts the container scale, record A and record B; the bytes are the same.

Broomy's research found on 4 October 2026 that the game seats Kliff's origin on the mount's B_Rider_01, 3.5 cm above where Seat rider on mount puts it, and the Speeder Bike's rider clips follow that. Speeder Bike's build reported on 5 October that the game does not carry him with the bone afterwards, so motion one clip has and the other lacks takes his hands off the grips. That disagrees with the test of 2 October under What shows in game on a mount, where Kliff moved with a float on B_Body_00, and no test has settled which holds. B_Rider_01's frame is turned against the world, its Y world up and its Z world forward, so an offset in the seat's frame is not along the world's axes.

## What shows in game on a mount

Bip01, the broom's root bone, shows its rotation but not all of its motion. The 75 degree climb tips the broom on Bip01 and that tilt is plain to see during Broomy's takeoff, yet a 25 degree roll and a 12 cm float with a 6 degree roll on Bip01 in the idle changed nothing while hovering. The climb's 5.3 m rise on Bip01 never shows as an offset either, so the root bone's translation goes into moving the broom. In flight Broomy's aim IK also works on the broom, which may be what flattens a Bip01 roll there.

B_Body_00, the bone under Bip01, does show. A 40 cm float with a 15 degree roll there showed on 2 October 2026, and Kliff moved with it: he sits on B_Rider_01, a child of B_Body_00, so a mount's idle motion goes on B_Body_00 and the rider's clip needs no matching edit. `examples/hover_idle.py` works that way. B_Rider_01 stays upright in every broom clip, so Kliff sits level however far Bip01 tips the broom.

An idle with motion on B_Body_00 jumps whenever the game restarts it with a short blend, as at the end of the takeoff. `examples/level_takeoff.py` makes a takeoff that holds the idle's first frame, so flight starts from the pose the takeoff ends in.

A chart node can hand a rider's limbs to IK with a frame event of type 0x65, 12 words. Words 3 to 6 name up to four limbs of the LimbIK modifier in `character/descriptors/posemodifierdata/posemodifierdata.xml` by hashlittle of the lower-case name, seed 0xC5EDE: FE419E03 lefthand, 017638FD righthand, A9203754 leftfoot, F41C56C3 rightfoot. For `phm_01` the left hand's limb pulls Bip01 L Hand so that B_IK_L_Hand meets B_TL_IK_L_Hand_00. A door opened with the left hand names lefthand alone, one opened with the right hand righthand, one opened with both both, and a ladder all four. The high half of word 7 and the low half of word 8 look like the first and second limbs' targets, by place in the chart's bone table, `strings[6]`, and the bones live on the mount. In `ride_test3_lower.paac` that table is B_IK_L_Hand, B_IK_R_Hand, bip_L_Reins and bip_R_Reins. A bear's node aims the hands at the first two, an mcb's at the reins, and the orca's and shark's underwater nodes aim the left hand at B_IK_L_Hand. The bear and machine bear skeletons have all four bones. The broom's, `cd_r0032_00_broom`, has nine bones and none of them. On 5 October 2026 the Speeder Bike mod, which rides the broom in the orca's state 01B8F8D3, had Kliff's left fist stuck at his belt whatever his clips did, and the 0x65 event in that node fits it. Its fix moves that node's instance of the event to 1000 s, so it never fires, and Seth saw the left hand on the grip in play at 08:34 on 5 October. An instance is 20 bytes: the time, an end time, the event's index, an ordinal and a word not yet known. This one is at 0x216BF in the shipped chart and starts 0, -1.0 and 777. Event 777 is shared by 77 states, and the fix moves only this one. The broom idle's node, 588C2001, has no 0x65, which is why Broomy's left hand follows the clip onto the handle. Type 0x5A, three words such as [0x5A, 0x20000, 3], is on most riding nodes and is not decoded.

The broom's rider template has no tracks for B_TL_IK_L_Hand_00, B_TL_IK_R_Hand_00, B_TL_IK_L_Foot_00 and B_TL_IK_R_Foot_00, so in every rider clip made from it they stay at rest, (0, 0.397, 1.388) in B_Rider_01's frame, which is Kliff's belt, where the Speeder Bike's left fist was stuck. Its rider clips key each onto its B_IK bone, within 0.2 mm read back, so LimbIK has nothing to move if a state turns it on. New rider clips could do the same by default.

## Writing a clip back

The exporter writes against the clip the action was imported from. It keeps that file's header, lead and block order, and fits new bones among the half float tracks, after the last one that comes before them in the skeleton. An imported clip exported without edits is byte for byte the original in all 44 cases, which `tests/check_export.py` checks.

Three things had to be exact for that:

- The axis corrections are 90 degree turns, so the conversion only moves and negates numbers. 189 rotation values in the samples are negative zero and survive the trip.
- The importer's sign flips are recorded per track (`neg` in the action's `cd_blocks`), and the exporter puts the stored signs back on those frames.
- The padding u16 of float position keys is the same in every key of a track in all 59 tracks that have it, so one value per track is kept.

Blender gives new keys Bezier interpolation, and the game only interpolates in straight lines. The exporter writes a track's keys as they are when every key is linear, and otherwise samples it at every frame, or every tick in a LOD's 8-bit blocks, from its first key to its last. The edited idle in the test turns Spine1 20 degrees and adds a new eyeball track with Bezier keys; it re-imports within 0.17 mm of the edited action.

## Baking constraints and IK

The exporter reads `rotation_quaternion` and `location` curves, which hold a bone's pose only when nothing else moves it. Before writing, it looks for bones that something else moves:

- any bone with an enabled constraint at an influence above 0;
- for IK and spline IK, every bone in the chain, `chain_count` bones up from the owner, or from the owner's parent for an IK with Use Tail off, or all the way to the root for 0;
- bones with a driver on any pose property;
- bones in a rotation mode other than quaternions, or keyed with Euler or axis-angle curves.

For those it plays the action frame by frame and takes each bone's evaluated pose in local space, with `Object.convert_space` from POSE to LOCAL. That is the space of its curves, on top of the bind pose and under the parent's evaluated pose, so a baked parent and an unbaked child still compose as they did in Blender. Quaternions are kept on one side from frame to frame. A bone gets rotation keys if it had rotation curves or turns at all, and position keys on the same rule. `tests/check_bake.py` puts four kinds of setup on the standing idle. Its re-imported clip is within 0.17 mm of the rigged pose, and the same export without baking misses by 77 cm.

The bake changes nothing in the scene. The frame, the active action and slot, and the NLA switch are restored after, and the rig keeps its constraints.

## IK controls

Add IK controls (`ik.py`) puts an IK constraint on each forearm and calf, two bones long, with a hand or foot control as its target and an elbow or knee control as its pole, and keys the controls so the clip's pose stays as it was.

Blender's IK puts the tail of the chain's last bone on the target. With Use Tail off the chain starts at the owner's parent instead, so on Kliff's hand it was his forearm's tail that met the target, and a chain of three moved his clavicle. Before 0.40.0 the bake counted such a chain from the owner and left its top bone out. It now starts at the parent. The game's limb bones are not joined. Kliff's hand starts 22.7 mm short of his forearm's tail and his foot 22.4 mm short of his calf's, both on the axis, so a target at the hand's head would leave the hand 2.3 cm off. The hand control sits at the forearm's tail instead, with the hand's turn. Of the 110 skeletons with Bip01 limbs, every hand and foot starts on its parent's axis within 0.007 mm except one of `m0001_00_circusmachine_boss`'s, 1.1 m off, which gets no control. The gap is 22.7 and 22.4 mm on `phm_01`, `ptm_01` and `ppdm_01`, 25.7 mm and none on `phw_01`, and none on most monsters.

The hand copies its control's whole transform, and a Copy Location then puts it back on the forearm at head/tail 1 + head.y / length, where the hand's own keys hold it. A Copy Rotation would do both in one, but it takes the rotation apart into Euler angles in the owner's rotation order, XYZ for a bone in quaternions. On frame 119 of the standing idle Kliff's right foot is at 90 degrees about Y in that order, which is gimbal lock, and Copy Rotation turned it 0.83 degrees from its control, 2.7 mm at the toe.

The solver's pole step, `ConstrainPoleVector` in `IK_QJacobianSolver`, turns the chain's first bone about the line from its head to the target until its axis X cos(a) + Z sin(a), a being the pole angle, points the way the pole does from that head, both taken square to the line. The add-on works out a from the clip as the way the elbow sticks out from the line from shoulder to wrist, in the upper arm's own X and Z, summed over the frames. Each frame's pole goes 40 cm out along that axis square to the line, level with the elbow, so the pole step leaves the clip's pose as it is. Kliff's four limbs all come out at 180 degrees, so his elbows and knees bend along the upper bone's -X. Speeder Bike's build found the same fitting its rider to the bike, where a pole angle of -180 degrees points Kliff's elbow at the pole and an arm IK with no pole hung the elbow straight down. A clip keyed later on the same armature keeps the first clip's pole angle, which changes only where its poles sit.

The solver starts from the bones' own keys, which stay on the limb, so the forearm keeps the clip's twist. The controls are keyed on every frame the clip keys a bone of the limb or one above it. On those frames the standing idle's arm and leg bones stay within 0.27 mm of the clip and the run's within 0.005 mm. Between keys the controls follow Blender's default interpolation while each bone of the clip blends its own rotation, and the idle's bones stray up to 1.4 mm from the clip there.

## Metadata on the action

Importing a full clip reads its `.paa_metabin` onto the action: the events and the phases, in seconds, stored as 32-bit floats like the file's own so an unedited clip writes the same numbers back. All 27 full clips in the samples come back byte for byte. The panel shows events in frames and snaps edits to half frames; every event in the shipped samples sits on a whole or half frame, such as 9.5 and 11.5 in `mount_l` and 14.5 in `dismount_r`.

Events may run past a clip's end. The standing idle's three events run to frame 250 of 240, so a clip cut shorter keeps events that end after it, as the shipped ones do. Phases appear only in mount and dismount clips, two in each dismount and one in `mount_r`. The add-on scales and keeps them but does not edit them, since what the two selectors and the vector mean is not known.

A new clip is a template imported under a new name. It keeps the template's header, lead, bone layout and metadata. The game only asks for a new name once an action chart points at it. Kliff's push-off was the first: on 3 October 2026 it played as `cd_phm_rd_broom_basic_00_00_nor_std_takeoff_00`, after the Broomy test build wrote that path into his lower chart as the file was read (the chart hashes its paths when it parses), with the loader serving the files through its stand-ins. Cutting it short trims each track at the new end and adds a key there holding the pose the template had on that frame. Holding the first frame leaves each track two keys with the same value, which the exporter writes as they are.

## The game's packs

Each group folder (0000 to 0035) has an index, 0.pamt, and data files 0.paz, 1.paz and so on. Clips, skeletons and blends are in group 0009 (549,104 of those files), clip metadata and action charts in 0010 (280,569). The index holds a header with the number of .paz files and their table, a tree of folder name fragments, a tree of file name fragments, folder records of 16 bytes (folder node, first file, file count, hash) and file records of 20 bytes (file name node, offset, stored size, size, flags). A file's path is its folder's path, a slash and its name, which gives the paths the game's logs print, such as `character/motion/1_pc/1_phm/00_riding/cd_phm_rd_broom_basic_00_00_nor_std_idle_01.paa`.

The parser in master looter's paz tools joins only the top folder to each name. That is not enough here: 26,553 clip names in 0009 are in more than one folder with different data, such as the mace kick in `1_pc/14_ptm/00_mon` and another folder. With full paths no two entries collide.

Flags bits 16 to 19 give the compression and a file is compressed when its stored size differs from its size. Type 2 is one LZ4 block. Type 1 keeps the file's own header and compresses what follows: a mesh stores each section of its PAR file as an LZ4 block, and a texture keeps its 128-byte DDS header and stores its mip levels in order, the first four reserved header words giving the stored size of the four largest. A level stored smaller than its real size is an LZ4 block; the smaller levels are plain. All 5,096 compressed textures in 0009 unpack to their stated size except three placeholder boxes in uncompressed RGBA. Bits 20 to 23 give the encryption. Only the XML-derived files, such as `.pac_xml`, are encrypted, all with type 3, ChaCha20 keyed by the file's name as `formats/chacha.py` describes, and decrypted before they are decompressed. The cipher and the LZ4 decoder are plain Python, since Blender ships neither.

## The search fields

Blender's search box filters and re-sorts the names a search field's callback returns, except when it first opens, which shows them as given (`editors/interface/interface_utils.cc`). The scoring is in blenlib's `string_search.cc`. It splits names and the typed text into words at spaces, dashes, underscores and slashes. A typed word scores 10 when it starts an unused word of the name, 4 when it matches word initials, and 3 less its errors when it sits inside a word or nearly matches one, and each pair of matched words out of the typed order loses 1. Of the best score it shows the name with the fewest letters first, and the other scores in the order given. So what reaches the box decides what shows. The add-on hands over at most 300 names, and before 0.40.2 those were the first 300 matches in the list's order. `idle` has 5,390 matches among Kliff's 199,424 clips, and his standing idle was not among the first 300. Since 0.40.2 the add-on ranks every match by the same rule and hands over the best 300, unless more than 20,000 match.

## Meshes

`formats/pac.py` has the full layout of a `.pac` mesh. The 40-byte vertex record follows CrimsonForge (github.com/hzeemr/crimsonforge, MIT), whose author read it from the game's skinning shader. The rest was read from the files on 3 October 2026 and checked on all 12,963 meshes in group 0009. CrimsonForge finds a mesh's parts by searching for byte patterns, the split between vertices and indices by scoring candidates, and the bone palette by scanning for hashes; all three are fixed fields here, apart from the palette on meshes with cloth data.

A mesh is a PAR file whose sections the packs store as LZ4 blocks inside it, compression type 1 in the index. Section 0 describes the parts and holds each detail level's vertex and index offsets; sections 1 to 4 are the levels. The vertex and index counts fill each level exactly. Each part has its own number of levels and a 4-byte map from the game's levels to its own, so an eye cover can have two while the head has three.

A vertex names up to six bones by slot. The slots index the mesh's palette, a list of bone name hashes, the same hash the clips use for tracks. Kliff's body has 189 and every one is a bone of `phm_01`. A flags word at the start of section 0 says which blocks come before the palette: a bounding box tree (0x2000), the mesh's own bone list (0x1) and cloth data (0x100, 0x200, 0x1000, 0x8000, 0x4), whose layout varies. Where only the tree comes first the palette's place follows from the layout; otherwise the reader takes the first u16 count followed by that many known bone hashes. On the 5,778 meshes where both apply they agree. Before 0.13.0 the reader refused 392 props whose palettes name bones of their own; `formats/pac.py` now reads the bone list, and of the 2,023 meshes with one only a sack flail object fails.

## Weapons and props

A mesh with bones of its own goes on an armature one of two ways. Armour such as helmets lists skeleton bones by name (Bip01, Bip01 Spine and on to Bip01 Head) with physics bones under them; each of its bones follows the armature's bone of the same name, or the nearest ancestor the armature has, and the mesh skins as any other. A weapon's root bone, such as B_Weapon_0001, is in no character skeleton, so the weapon goes to a socket and follows that socket's bone rigidly.

`formats/sockets.py` has the formats. A skeleton's socket file lists sockets as a parent bone and a transform in its frame; phm_01 has 52. The same file names some sockets again under StackEquipInfo, with no transform, for equipment stacked over clothes; only the SocketList defines sockets. A weapon's prefab names a socket file of its own, whose sockets are on the weapon. The character descriptions give each part, by its prefab component name, a socket and a weapon socket for use (Out) and for put away (In): CD_MainWeapon_Sword_R goes to RHand_Socket by the sword's Basic_ChildSocket, its grip, and to Pelvis_L_Socket by its Pelvis_L_ChildSocket when sheathed. The weapon is placed so that its socket lands on the character's: placement = character socket at rest times the inverse of the weapon socket in the weapon's space. The Blender bone is the game bone turned by its axis correction, which the socket's frame undoes.

The descriptions are named by creature type (phm_description_player_kliff, phm_description_001, animal_wolf, mon_wyvern), and a character's record in the data tables names its own (see Gear below). The add-on reads that file first, then the rest of the 121, the skeleton's family first (phm for phm_01) and, within it, the player files first for a playable character and last for anyone else. The first file to define a part gives its whole rule. Merging attribute by attribute, as 0.13.0 did, let the player file's Visible="Out" reach an NPC's shield and hid it.

Visible="Out" appears 285 times, 136 of them in the three player files. phm_description_player_kliff marks the shield, bow, quiver, dagger and most other weapons, so the player's spare weapons show only in hand. The sword has no Visible and stays at the hip. WeaponCasePart names the scabbard's component, CD_MainWeapon_Sword_IN_R for the sword.

A StackEquipInfo group names an origin bone, an axis in that bone's game frame, the inner parts it stacks over and its sockets. phm_01's Back group pushes Bip_Weapon_Attach_In_02 along z over CD_Cloak and CD_Upperbody for the five back sockets; Pelvis_L and Pelvis_R push the hip sockets over CD_Upperbody. How far the game moves them is not in the file. The add-on measures, at rest, how much further the inner parts stand out than the bare body along the axis within 10 cm of the axis line, and moves equipment at the group's sockets by that much: 12.1 cm on Kliff's back, where his cloak hangs loose, 8.6 cm on a south soldier, about 1 cm at the hips. Arms hanging beside the hips fall inside the 10 cm, but in the inner parts and the body alike, so they mostly cancel; the sleeves add the 1 cm.

## Chains

A mesh's bones that the armature lacks were mapped to their nearest ancestor before 0.15.0 and moved rigidly. `chains.py` now adds them to the armature as `<mesh>:<bone>` under the bone they hang from, a socket's bone for a weapon, with cd_extra set. Export and the IK bake skip any bone without the skeleton's cd_index, so they never reach a clip.

Which bones the game moves with physics is not in the files the add-on reads. The `meshphysics/*.hkx` file of a weapon or cloak holds only convex hulls (hknpConvexShape) for the item lying on the ground. The character's `havokphysics/*.hkx` is a ragdoll, capsules on the main bones and their constraints (hknpRagdollData). The prefab has no physics component, and the materials are SkinnedMeshStandard. Cloaks such as Kliff's carry no bones of their own; their cloth is in the Havok data after the mesh's palette, which the add-on does not read. So the add-on picks chains by name: a run of bones named as one family with a number, each the parent of the next, whose family says chain (not chainsaw), flail, rope, whip or feather. Of the 2,023 meshes with bones of their own, 88 have one: 67 hanging chains, 9 flails, 7 long flails, 2 ropes, the flexible flail, a whip and a feathered helmet. Numbered families that are not chains, B_Add_Arrow_Down on 24 bows, B_HandlePos on greatswords, B_Hel_Eye on visors, B_Att_Clock on the flight cloak's wings, stay rigid.

The first bone of a chain holds on to its parent. Each later one is a link turning about a pivot. Usually that is its head. The short flail's chain and ball are one rigid piece on B_Add_Flail_002, whose head is at the ball's centre, 1.524 m down the weapon, so it would swing about the ball. Where a bone's vertices touch its parent's, within 2 cm, the pivot is where they meet instead: z -0.879 for that flail, the stick's tip. The flexible flail's links blend between bones and its heads are the joints. A link's tail is the next link's pivot, or at the end of a chain the middle of its own vertices, the ball. Its mass is its vertex count, and an end piece's radius is the 90th percentile of its vertices' distance from that middle.

`bake()` is a position-based simulation at 120 steps a second. Each step moves the tails under gravity, damps their speed relative to the pelvis's, then ten times pulls each link back to its length, the end with less mass giving way more, and pushes the tails out of capsules around the body's main bones and above the lowest foot or toe bone. Damping relative to the hand's speed, tried first, pumped energy into the chains with every arm swing; damping against the world dragged a running character's chains back as if into a headwind. Solving the links one after another from the root let the light links yank the heavy ball; weighting by mass keeps the ball steady. A capsule's radius is the median distance from its bone of the vertices of CD_Nude whose strongest group is that bone or a helper under it (Calf_Sub, UpArmTwist), since the skin is weighted mostly to helpers: on Kliff 5.8 cm at the calf, 17 cm at the chest. Before the first frame a chain settles for three seconds at that frame's pose and then starts with the speed of what carries it. In a run with damping 0.03 the links rise at most 33 cm above their pivots; at 0.1 at most 12 cm.

The keys go into an action of their own, marked cd_chains, on an NLA track called CD chains, under the clip's action, which has no chain bones. Importing a clip onto an armature with chains bakes again over the clip's frames.

What is left out: a chain does not collide with itself, the weapon, the clothes or another chain; the game's own chain settings, if any, are not known.

The mesh is in the skeleton's bind space, the space the armature's rest pose is built in, so the importer adds no offset. Swapping Y and Z into Blender mirrors it, so each triangle gets two corners swapped to keep facing outward; 99.8% of Kliff's triangles and all of the Wyvern's then face the way the file's normals do. Weights add up to 255 on 96% of vertices outside cloth meshes and are scaled to a sum of 1. The Havok data after the palette is not read.

## Materials and textures

A mesh `character/model/X.pac` has its materials in `character/modelproperty/X.pac_xml`, one entry per part keyed by the part's name, which matched all 587 parts of 200 sampled meshes. An entry names a shader and its parameters: textures by game path, floats, colours as `#rrggbbaa`, and four-byte values written as one integer. A parameter left at the shader's default is not written. Some files hold several `ModelProperty` entries with the same parts; these are colour variants. 99.9% of the 71,086 texture references in 1,500 sampled files are in group 0009 under `character/texture/`.

The material map (`_sp`) was read from the textures. On a sword handle, B is 1 on the gold wire and 0 on the leather, and G drops on the wire: B is metallic and G roughness. On Kliff's head B is bright only in the ears, so on skin shaders B goes to subsurface. R is near 1 on hard surfaces and holds pore detail on skin; the add-on uses it as specular level, which is a guess.

Normal maps are DirectX style. Wherever a height map exists for comparison, red follows minus the height's slope across the texture and green minus its slope down the rows, the same sign for both on every sampled pair, so green points down the texture and is inverted for Blender.

Eyes keep the iris and pupil in textures of their own. Both use the eyeball's UV square: the iris texture's alpha is the iris disc and the pupil texture's alpha the pupil disc, so the add-on lays them over the eyeball by those alphas. Kliff's eye cover, the film over the eyes, names only a normal map; with no alpha texture the add-on makes the cover clear.

Two-sided cards, such as eyebrows, list some triangles twice, once per side. Blender holds one face per set of corners and draws both sides, so the importer drops the repeat; Kliff's eyebrows had 80 of 4,592.

Hair and beard textures are near white; `_hairDyeingColor`, a sandy brown for Kliff, multiplies them.

The layered shaders, names ending in `_Ver2` and about 40% of all materials, have no base colour. A mask texture (`_ma`) splits the part into layers by its R, G and B channels. Each layer names a tint colour and a set of small tiling textures from a shared library (`cd_texturelayer_*`). Layer R's tiling comes from `_dyeingTransformProperty0`, G's from 1 and B's from 3: in 1,500 files, a part with only layer R writes only property 0, one with R and G writes 0 and 1. The first byte is the tile size as a fraction of the UV square, so a layer repeats 255 / byte times. Across 5,776 uses, the same tiling texture gets a smaller byte on meshes with more metres per UV unit (log-log slope -0.22), which only holds if a smaller byte means more repeats. A second mask (`_mg`) holds edge wear in R and grime in G, and grime and scratch textures and colours go with it. How the game's shader combines all this is not known. The add-on multiplies each layer's tint by its tiling colour texture, mixes the layers by the mask, takes roughness and metal from each layer's tiling material map, and leaves out grime, scratches, dye and the detail normals.

## Writing a mesh for the game

The add-on does not write meshes, but the Speeder Bike mod writes a Sketchfab model into the broom's `.pac`, and two fixes made it look like the game's own models in game on 5 October 2026. Its scripts are `speeder/build_mesh.py` and `speeder/make_textures.py` in that mod.

Normals turn with the positions. A Blender object's world matrix moves the positions, and the normals need the inverse transpose of its 3x3, normalized, then the same swap to game space as the positions. Normals written untouched were in the wrong axes, and the model showed very dark with blotchy shadows. A check needs no game. For each triangle, the face normal from the game's winding should agree with the mean of its three vertex normals. On the broom's own mesh all of them agree, 5.8 degrees apart at the median. The untouched normals agreed on 43.9% of triangles, 73 degrees apart, and the turned ones on all of them, 6.9 degrees apart.

Hard surfaces in the game are rough and not metallic. The `_sp` maps of the wagon `cd_r0004_02_wagon_0002_sp`, the machine tank's `cd_m0001_00_machinetank_0001_hel_0002_00_01_01_sp` and the broom `cd_t0000_broom_0001_sp` have medians of R 255, 255 and 0, G 231, 247 and 243, and B 0 in all three. Their base colours sit at 56 to 67 sRGB luminance at the 10th percentile and 82 to 92 at the median. The Sketchfab model's set was glossy metal over a near-black base, 9 at the 10th percentile with G 88 and B 37. It rendered near black under the game's lighting, while Blender's bright sky showed it as silver. The speeder's textures now have metal 0, roughness remapped linearly into 140 to 255 and R at 255. Its base blacks are lifted to 40 sRGB, as 40 + v * 215 / 255, and only 40% of the baked ambient occlusion is multiplied in.

The vertex record's tangent is not known. On the broom, bytes +6 and +7 and bits 0 to 9 of the normal word at +16 vary from vertex to vertex, and no tangent layout tried came out perpendicular to the normal. Bytes +12 to +15 are always 00 00 00 3C, which is the second texture coordinate pair at 0 and 1 in half floats. The speeder writes zeros in +6, +7 and bits 0 to 9 with a flat BC5 normal map, which worked in game, and leaves the model's own normal map unused.

## Characters

A skeleton's clips are in its folder under `character/motion/` named as under `character/model/`: `1_pc/1_phm/` holds 199,424 clips for phm_01 and `2_mon/cd_m0004_00_dragon/cd_m0004_00_wyvern/` 215 for the Wyvern. 124 of the 273 skeletons have no clips in that folder; for some, such as the hexe wolf, the snow golem and the cliff crab, no clip in the game names the creature at all.

Writing a new scene with `bpy.data.libraries.write` crashes Blender 5.2 (an access violation in `BKE_view_layer_copy_data`) unless the scene's view layer has been updated first, which Build characters does before saving each character.

A character's look is an appearance file, `character/appearance/<group>/<name>.app_xml`, encrypted like the material files. It lists prefabs in sections (Nude, Head, Hair, Armor), some with attributes such as CharacterScale and HeadScale, and some outfit prefabs marked Preview. Kliff's is `cd_phm_macduff_00000`: the macduff appearances use `meshparam_example_kliff.xml` and the voice event `vce_pc_kliff`.

A prefab, `character/bin__/prefab/.../<name>.prefab`, is a binary reflection dump of a scene object. Its type table names the fields (SkinnedMeshComponent, _skinnedMeshFile, _modelPropertyIndex and so on), and each component stores its name followed by the bytes 01 01 00 01 03 and then, after a u32 length, its mesh's game path. `formats/appearance.py` reads only those two strings. The field values are written sparsely, so the variant index `_modelPropertyIndex` is not read and every part uses variant 0.

The appearance lists Kliff's plain inner layers first (`cd_phm_00_ub_inner_0054` and others) and the outfit after them, and each outfit component carries the same name as the inner one it covers, such as CD_Upperbody. Applying prefabs in order with a later component replacing an earlier one of the same name gives his default look. Of 5,667 appearance files, one is not well-formed XML; the rest resolve to 42,013 meshes, all in the packs. Hidden components are those named for the flight cloak, the hyperspace plug line and docking bags.

The body prefab's data file, `character/prefab/.../<name>.prefabdata_xml`, names the skeleton (`1_pc/1_phm/phm_01.pab` for Kliff) and a skeleton variation, `cd_phm_00_nude_01_0002.pabc`; the head prefab's names the face's, `cd_phm_macduff_head_0001.pabc`. The 0.92 head scale in the appearance is not applied.

## Skeleton variations

`formats/pabc.py` has the layout. Each record is a bone hash and three matrices: the world bind with scale, the local without scale, and a packed form holding rotation, position, scale and the inverse of the parent's scale. Chaining the packed form as parent world, times the inverse parent scale, times the bone's own transform gives the world matrix to 0.01 mm for most bones, but some records hang from another parent than the skeleton's: Kliff's clavicles from the neck instead of Spine2, the tongue from the lower teeth. The add-on uses the world matrix.

Kliff's body variation moves no main joint. It scales bones across their length, thighs 1.12 thicker, the spine 0.91, the shoulder muscle helpers up to 1.66, and moves and turns helper bones by up to 31 mm; the head variation moves face bones by up to 14 mm. Over the 97 male variations, moves reach 163 mm and scales 1.87.

The inverse parent scale in every record means children ignore their parent's scale, so a bone's scale shapes only its own vertices. The importer builds the rest pose from the variation's world matrices without scale, so clips sit on the character's own bind; keys need no change and export is untouched. Meshes are stored in the shared skeleton's bind space. In the game a vertex is skinned by its bones' posed matrices, each with the bone's own scale, times the inverse of the shared bind. That equals Blender's skinning times a fixed offset per bone, the variation's scaled world bind times the inverse of the shared one. Blender cannot hold an offset per bone, so the importer applies the weighted offsets to each vertex once, at import. At rest that is exact; under a pose it differs where a vertex follows bones whose offsets differ and that bend against each other. In a dismount Kliff's body is within 6.5 mm of the game's skinning on every vertex, 99% within 4.1 mm.

CharacterScale, 1.02571 for Kliff, becomes the armature object's scale.

## Cloth

The low 6 bits of a vertex record's last byte (+39) are 63 on a vertex the skeleton moves alone and 0 on one the game's cloth simulation moves. On Kliff's leather cloak, cd_phm_00_cloak_0054_15, 573 of 1,990 vertices are 0, all of them from about 1.4 m down; the 725 vertices above 1.5 m are all 63, and the band between holds 31, 15 and 14. Every vertex of his body is 63. Before 0.16.0 the reader read this byte as saying whether the fifth and sixth weights count; they do only on 63, so the reader's weights are unchanged. In 700 sampled character meshes, 275 have a vertex below 63: 95 of 111 upper bodies (coat tails), 42 of 49 cloaks, vests, belts, necklaces, helmets with cloth, flags, and 9 of 10 hair meshes. Hair runs from 63 at the root down to as low as 13 at the tips with no 0; on Kliff's hair 24,607 of 27,364 vertices are below 63. A mesh with such vertices carries cloth flags (0x100, 0x200, 0x1000, 0x8000) and a block of 16-byte records before its palette, 70 KB on the leather cloak, not decoded; the Havok tag file after the bounding box is again only a convex hull.

`cloth.py` gives each part with a vertex at 0 to Blender's cloth: the part moves to an object of its own, the Cloth modifier comes after the Armature modifier so the skinned shape is the cloth's goal, and the pin group is the value over 63. The game's meshes split vertices along texture seams, so the cloth object's vertices that share a spot are joined; left split, a free panel had no hold on the collar and fell away. Pieces that still touch no pinned vertex are pinned whole: 195 eight-vertex fur strands on Kliff's black cloak, a 60-vertex strip of his coat, a scabbard's tassel. In the game they must follow the cloth through data the add-on does not read, perhaps the fifth and sixth weight slots, which free vertices do not use for weights. The short and long flails' chain and ball are free vertices too, and the flexible flail's are not; a part whose free vertices mostly hang on a swinging chain bone stays with the chain.

The settings are one set for heavy fabric: quality 6, mass 0.4, air damping 1.5, tension and compression 20, shear 10, bending 1, collision distance 1 cm against the body's Collision modifier (outer thickness 1.5 cm), no self collision. On Kliff, the leather cloak (1,847 vertices after joining) and his coat's tails take about 0.25 s a frame to simulate together. In a run the hem moves up to 54 cm off the skinned shape and nothing goes further than 0.72 m from the pelvis; standing, the cloak sags up to 5.4 cm from its modelled shape.

## Hair

Blender's cloth on Kliff's hair took 133 s for 49 frames, 2.7 s a frame, because the hair is 27,364 vertices of cards. `sway.py` gives it spring bones instead. Each hair vertex below 63 is shared between the two sides of the head it lies between, by its angle around Bip01 Head, and gets 1 - v / 63 of its weight on those sides' sway bones; its skeleton weights are scaled down by the same amount. A side's bone runs from the middle of its roots, the vertices at 63, to the weighted middle of its swaying vertices, so Kliff's four are 4.7 to 6.9 cm long and Damian's 23 to 29 cm.

`chains.bake()` moves a bone with `cd_stiffness` as a damped spring toward the tip the head carries, with no gravity. The stiffness is 2g / length, a pendulum half the bone's length, so the swing angle for a given jolt of the head is about the same on short and long hair. The damping is half of critical and works on the speed relative to that tip, not to the pelvis as a chain's does. A cone keeps the bone within 45 degrees of the tip. A first try at a fixed 1.5 swings a second sent Kliff's 5 cm bones past 140 degrees, because a fixed spring lets a jolt move the tip the same distance on any bone. A bug in that try measured the pull from where the bone was, not from where it was heading, so a bone running with the head sat one substep ahead of it, 3.8 cm at Kliff's running speed, whatever the stiffness. With both fixed, Kliff's run swings his hair at most 23 degrees and Damian's 13; their idles stay under a degree.

The game's own hair settings are not found. The vertex values say how much each strand may move, not how stiff it is.

Each sway bone hangs from the bone that carries most of its swaying vertices, weighting each vertex's skin weights by its sway share. That is Bip01 Head or Bip01 T Head for Kliff's hair, a forehead bone for some fringes, Bip01 Spine2_Sub for all four of Damian's springs, since that long hair rests on the upper back, and a face bone under B_face_com for a beard. The face rig has no jaw bone; a grid of bones under B_face_com, some named B_Forehead although they sit at the chin (B_Forehead_15_C is at the chin's tip), carries the beard, and facial clips move them, so a beard spring on one follows the mouth.

Of the game's 109 beard meshes, 47 have swaying vertices, 4 of them among the 15 `_player` versions. Kliff's cd_phm_00_beard_00_0005_06_player and Oongka's cd_phm_00_beard_00_0003_05_player are held whole, although the NPC version of Oongka's, cd_phm_00_beard_00_0003_05, sways 18,819 of its 20,582 vertices. The southern bandits wear swaying ones: phm_south_23002 has cd_phm_00_beard_00_0003 and phm_south_32032 the mustache cd_phm_00_beard_00_0005_07. A beard's sides have swaying strands and few roots of their own, since it is rooted around the mouth, so a side with no roots starts at its swaying vertices' nearest roots, weighted by sway share. The first sway bones started such a side at the head bone, inside the skull. Nearest roots do not work for hair: its cards lie in layers, every swaying vertex has a root just under it, and the springs came out under 2 cm long, so hair keeps the middle of its side's roots. In a run the 23002 beard swings up to 29 degrees, Kliff's hair 23.

## Facial clips

Kliff's skeleton folder, `1_pc/1_phm`, has 142,527 facial clips: `99_autofacial/<language>` (14,598 to 23,371 lip sync clips in each of eight languages, named by speaker such as `unique_kliff_...` and `nhm_adult_...`), `99_arfacial` (3,896, cutscene faces named by sequence and date), `99_facial/00_action` (81) and `99_facial/99_emotion` (46). Damian's `2_phw` folder has autofacial, arfacial and emotion clips too, fewer of each, and `14_ptm` autofacial ones. One clip of each family keys 193 to 200 tracks, all on B_face_com and the bones under it, mostly position keys: on the angry face 125 tracks with rotation and position and 74 with position only, on a spoken line 141 with position only. None of 400 sampled body clips in `1_pc/1_phm` keys a face bone. So a body clip and a facial clip never fight over a bone, and the add-on layers the facial clip on an NLA track, `CD face`, with Replace blending, under the active body action.

The face bones' keys are offsets from the shared skeleton's bind, like every other key. Played on the shared skeleton and on Kliff with his head variation, a clip's frame 0 sits the same distance from each one's own rest: a median 2.99 mm and at most 20 mm for the generic calm face. The `_kliff` versions are not made on Kliff's variation either. Frame 0 of the `_kliff` calm face is 0.94 mm from the shared rest, while his variation moves face bones a median 4.29 mm and up to 14.4 mm from it. So a facial clip moves each character's own face and keeps its shape.

Blender does not reset a channel when the NLA stops animating it, so after a facial clip's last frame the face keeps that frame's pose; the strip says so with Hold Forward. Remove facial clip deletes the track and puts the face bones back on rest.

On the bandit phm_south_23002, the beard's springs hang on B_Forehead_15_C, which a spoken line moves up to 34 mm; the springs turn at most 6.3 degrees. The add-on does not load the voice audio of the spoken lines.

## Voice

The game's sound is Wwise. There are 390,308 `.wem` files and 3,288 `.bnk` banks. Group 0004 holds the banks and `sound/windows/media/`, numbered files for sound effects and music plus 25,511 numbered files in each language folder under it. The spoken lines are in one group per installed language, named: 0006 is `sound/windows/english(us)/` (60,461 lines), 0005 Korean, 0035 Chinese. A voice file's name is the name of its lip sync clip, so `99_autofacial/english(us)/<line>` plays `sound/windows/english(us)/<line>.wem`. For Kliff's skeleton 15,557 of the 21,413 English lip sync clips have one, 1,851 of Kliff's own 2,869. Three of Kliff's voice files are named by a number, such as `unique_kliff_15286332413776243713`, and the facial clips of those three have the same numbered names.

Every voice file is Wwise Vorbis (codec 0xFFFF), mono at 48 kHz, with a 0x42-byte fmt chunk holding the Vorbis header and mod packets (signal 0xCB). Its setup packet names its codebooks by 10-bit index into Wwise's aoTuV 6.03 table, so the conversion needs that table; `formats/wwise.py` rebuilds the three Vorbis headers and the audio packets the way ww2ogg does. ww2ogg writes granule position 0 on every page of these files, which have none; the port counts the samples from the block sizes and puts the file's sample count on the last page, so players read the true length. 200 random English lines all convert, Blender's audio library and ffmpeg decode them without error to exactly the sample count in the file, and their levels are speech (one: mean -26 dB, peak -8 dB).

The missing lines are not in the installed files. Every language has them: 16% of Kliff's skeleton's Chinese lip sync clips have no voice, 27% of English, 31% of Korean, and up to 43% for `14_ptm`, with DLC lines worst (1,205 of 2,935 English DLC clips). Of the 4,681 English scenes, 3,222 are fully voiced, 441 partly, with gaps scattered through them, and 1,018 not at all. Nothing else in the files holds them:

- The numbered files in `sound/windows/media/<language>/` are efforts and grunts. They are 44.1 kHz where dialogue is 48 kHz, every language has its own 25,511 with no ID shared, and the `dialogvoiceinfo` table maps the 519 voice types (`Unique_Kliff`, `Unique_Damian`) to effort events such as `vce_effort_kliff`.
- The 325 banks per language hold 55,339 HIRC objects, 34,503 of them sounds and 484 events, and no line name hashes to an event, sound or media ID under FNV-1, FNV-1a or hashlittle (seed 0 or 0xC5EDE), with or without `play_`, `vo_` or a path.
- Matching on the name without its speaker type finds 492 more English files, but many are another character's voice for the same line: Oongka's face clip with Kliff's voice, Shane's with Dwayne's, Laurent's with Astin's. Among lines that do match exactly, 77 in 2,000 would also match some other file this way. The add-on does not guess.
- The game's group list in `meta/0.papgt` names only the installed groups; 0018 and 0034 are absent from it and from the folder.

So the game ships lip sync for more lines than it voices, and voices about 42,000 lines per language, battle calls and ambient talk among them, that have no lip sync clip. When a line has no voice in its own language, the add-on checks the other installed ones: 438 English misses for Kliff's skeleton are voiced in Korean and 60 in Chinese, and the warning names that language's lip sync clip, which plays the line with its own lips.

Lip sync clips end on a whole frame. The voice runs on past the clip. In 80 matched lines it ran on a median 51 ms and at most 86 ms, and one clip was 1.9 s longer than its line; in 45 more it ran on 34 to 66 ms. Both start at frame 0; whether the game starts them together is not checked.

## Sending clips to the game

The loader logs a session to `CDAnimator.log`, renaming the one before to `CDAnimator.01.log` at startup, so the log file's creation time is the session's start; its lines carry the time of day only. It logs a `[folder]` line for each file it found at startup and a `[files]` line the first time the game takes each, with the game's path. In the first test the game took both wait faces' metadata and `_lod.paa` while the save loaded, then each `.paa` when Kliff first played it, 42 and 59 s after startup. The loader walks its folder depth first in directory order, so of two same-named files in different folders the one whose path sorts first wins; Send to game refuses rather than guess.

Clip logging (`LogClips=1`) writes a `[clip]` line the first time a session loads each clip or blend, with its path under `character/motion/`, and a `---- N s with no new clip ----` marker before the first one after 2 s with none. The session in the second jaw test logged 3,174 lines in 8 moments: 2 clips as the game started, the save's waves of 1,306, 665 and 227 clips (1,215 and 643 of the first two are Kliff's skeleton's), then Kliff's desert fidget start and loop, the generic wait face with a raccoon's idle, and his own wait face, a few seconds apart.

An open mouth needs the teeth and tongue. The face rig has no jaw bone; in Kliff's speech the lower teeth bone B_Toothdown_C turns about 3 degrees and moves 4.9 mm at the widest, the tongue bones turn up to 13 degrees, and the lip, inner lip and chin bones move 12 to 15 mm. A first test moved only the 33 face bones below the mouth and in front of the head down 15 mm. In game the lower lip stretched while the teeth stayed shut, and it read as swollen lips. Adding twice the speech frame's pose of every face bone instead opened the mouth with the teeth apart, in Blender and in game.

Export writes its own `_lod.paa`, so even an unchanged clip's LOD differs from the game's: 828 bytes for Kliff's idle against the game's 572. The two wait faces' LODs came out 86 bytes each with no tracks, the same as the game's own: facial clips' LODs are empty, so the game does not animate faces far from the camera. Kliff's idle LOD has the game's 28 tracks with 88 keys against the game's 42, since the export thins keys less. In 60 sampled facial and 60 body clips the export's LOD keeps every track the game's has. The clip and its metadata are byte for byte the game's.

## Action charts

The game has 558 action charts (`.paac`, group 0010, under `actionchart/bin__/`), and no two share a file name, so the loader can serve each by name. `formats/paac.py` is the reader Broomy's research wrote from the game's chart loader (Broomy's chart schema has every address). It parses 556 of them and packs each back byte for byte; `commonactioninfo` and its `_serialized` copy have a count the reader rejects.

A node names its clip by two u16 indexes into the chart's second string table, `anim_index` at 0x92 of its header and `anim_index_a` at 0x90, equal in all 105,586 nodes, so the add-on sets both. The table holds paths under `character/motion/` with `.paa` and without the trailing zero, which the reader strips. The game hashes the path when it loads the chart and does not check that the clip exists. A new clip's path goes at the end of the table, so the game's own paths keep their indexes and every other node and record of the chart stays byte for byte the same. Taking a clip out sets its nodes back to the game chart's indexes and drops added paths no node plays, which renumbers only paths after them.

Kliff's standing idle, `cd_phm_basic_00_00_nor_std_idle_00`, plays at 101 nodes in 21 charts. Node 0 of `basic_upper.paac` is the whole of action 26107D3C, which is that chart's default action. In `basic_lower.paac` the same key has two nodes, Kliff's idle and the female idle `1_pc/2_phw/cd_phw_basic_00_00_normal_stand_idle_000`, and the chart's default is 866C7489.

An action's key is `hashlittle(name, 0xC5EDE)`. Hashing every string in every chart's tables names 136 of the 84,133 actions, and their own chart's name table names 37, so the add-on shows actions by key.

The loader already finds clips no pack has: the game's find and existence checks for a file in its folder that no pack holds go to a stand-in of the same kind, Kliff's broom idle, its LOD, its metadata or his riding blend, and the read then gets the folder's bytes. That first worked on 3 October with Kliff's push-off, which the Broomy test build named from his lower chart in memory. Broomy found that the game asks for a new clip's metadata too, so a new clip needs all three files. Broomy noted that the chart loader frees a chart's buffer with the plain heap free and crashed on a buffer its plugin allocated. The loader allocates the replacement with the game's own allocator, as it does for clips, and charts from its folder load without a crash. In the first test on 3 October, `examples/new_name.py` pointed Kliff's idle action at `cd_phm_cdanim_raised_idle_00`, and he stood with his arms raised. The log shows `basic_upper.paac` and `basic_lower.paac` taken from the folder 23 s after startup, the new clip's metadata and LOD found through their stand-ins while the save loaded, and the clip itself 29 s after startup. Broomy patches some of Kliff's charts in memory after reading them, `ride_upper.paac` among them. Whether its patches still land on a copy of one of those from the loader's folder is not tested, so those charts are best left to Broomy.

Charts name 23,261 of the 56,897 body clips in Kliff's skeleton folder (facial and lip sync clips left out). Blends name 4,730 more, among them about half his walk and run clips. The other 28,900, 19,597 of them under `02_mission`, are named by something not yet looked at, probably the sequencer and mission files.

The game has 1,688 blends (`.motionblending`, group 0009, under `character/binary/motionblending/`). `formats/motionblend.py` is Broomy's codec, which writes all 1,688 back byte for byte and recomputes the offsets and footers its elements carry, so a path can change length. A blend's clips are its `_animationFileNames`, paths under `character/motion/` with `.paa`, and each example names one by index. In all 1,688 no clip is listed twice, so a new clip takes the template's slot and the list keeps its length. Charts name 1,206 of the blends by full path, in their sixth string table, through a node's `blend_index_a` and `blend_index_b`.

44 blend names belong to two blends each, all of them different files: the male and female locomotion blends (`phm_locomotion` and `phw_locomotion`) and the item blends with their `sequencer` copies. The loader matched files by name only, so a male blend in its folder would have gone to female characters too. Loader 0.2.0 serves a file under `CDAnimator/by_path` for the game path it sits at and nothing else, and the add-on puts every chart and blend it edits there. It reads the loader's version from `CDAnimator.asi`, where 0.2.0 writes it as one string, and refuses with an older loader.

Kliff's run, `cd_phm_basic_00_00_nor_move_run_f_ing_00`, is slot 0 of `basic_move_lv3` and of `basic_move_lv3_new`, and chart nodes in `basic_lower.paac` play it straight too. The game loads both blends while a save loads, so the log does not say which one runs. In the test on 3 October, `examples/new_name.py` put `cd_phm_cdanim_raised_run_00` in slot 0 of both, and Kliff ran with his arms raised. The log shows both male blends taken from `by_path` while the save loaded, the female `phw_locomotion/basic_move_lv3` loaded in the same millisecond and left the game's, and the new run and its LOD found through their stand-ins. The game never asked for the run's metadata, though it asked for the idle's, which a chart plays, so clips a blend plays seem to go without it.

A move is a new action. In all 556 charts the reader parses, the actions are sorted by key, so the add-on looks for a name `CDA_Move_<n>` whose key is larger than every key in the chart and puts the action last; no index of the game's actions moves. The action is a copy of Kliff's standing idle, 26107D3C, with one new node for the clip. Its only exit is the idle's end branch (kind 7), back to the idle when the clip ends. With all of the idle's exits it left again in the same frame, because standing still already meets some of their conditions. The key branch is a copy of a plain key branch of the same chart, one key condition and nothing else, aimed at the new key. Every action that plays the idle clip gets it as its first slot, with its own slots copied after it to the end of the slot table.

A key condition is the token `[0xE9, index of the input's name in strings[0], hashlittle(name), 0, 0]` with the input's name in lower case, `key_cda_<clip>` for a move. The input is `Key_CDA_<clip>` in the CharacterAction group of `ui/inputmap.xml` (group 0012, with a BOM and CRLF line ends), `<KeyboardMouse Key="l" Method="downonce"/>`. E did not work as a move's key in the first test: at idle with no target the game never raises `key_interaction`. K played a move on 4 October but opened the skill view too: `ui/inputmap.xml` binds only five groups (housing, two camera groups, CharacterAction and camera functions), and the menus' keys are in `ui/inputmap_common.xml`, where K is HudSkillViewOpen, J the journal, I the inventory and M the map. Some entries list a whole keyboard row, such as `f g h j k l ; &quot;`; those are not bindings. Counting a Key of up to three names as a binding (`shift alt f10` is one), the keys neither file binds are L, 0, 7, 8, 9, F4, F5, F6, F12, End, Insert, left Ctrl and the numpad's 0 to 9.

Each character runs two charts, layer 0 the upper chart and layer 1 the lower. A move in `basic_upper` alone was taken on L, but the lower layer's idle covered it, and on Space the lower chart's jump hid it. In both charts it plays. ## Event types

The exe names the parts of a `.paa_metabin` next to each other: `AnimationMetaData`, `AnimationFrameTag` with `_beginAnimTime`, `_endAnimTime` and `_animationFrameTagType`, and `FootStepInfo` with `_footStepType`, `_footIndex`, `_velocity`, `_footStepVelocity` and `_footPosition`. An event is a frame tag. The tag type is a plain enum with no value names in the exe, so the meanings below come from the clips. `UseFootPlantingByFrameTag` is among the exe's strings, so the game plants feet by these tags.

Over all 280,011 metadata files there are 2,089,764 events. They come in left and right pairs. 4 and 5 are long windows, and in 214,143 clips each covers the whole clip. 2 and 3 run about 5 to 9 frames, 6 and 7 one frame, and 0 and 1 alternate so that together they cover the clip. The commonest sets are 1, 4 and 5 (176,959 clips, the standing ones) and all eight (28,264).

Playing 120 of Kliff's clips with foot tags on his skeleton and measuring the feet inside each type's windows:

| Type | Left foot | Right foot | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0.41 m/s | 1.49 m/s | left foot planted |
| 5 | 1.51 m/s | 0.41 m/s | right foot planted |
| 2 | 2.96 m/s, 51 cm up | 0.53 m/s | left foot swinging |
| 3 | 0.48 m/s | 3.09 m/s | right foot swinging |
| 6 | 3.66 m/s | 0.77 m/s | left step, one frame |
| 7 | 0.78 m/s | 3.53 m/s | right step, one frame |

Frame by frame, 6 sits where the foot is highest: in a panic turn the left foot leaves the ground at frame 10, tag 6 is on frames 13 and 14 at its 12 cm top, and tag 2 runs from there to touchdown at frame 17, where tag 4 begins. Types 0 and 1 barely differ in the feet: the left foot is 12 cm ahead in 0 windows and the right 7 cm ahead in 1, and whole runs carry 0 while idles carry 1, so neither "which foot leads" nor "moving or standing" fits; they stay unnamed.

A phase is a `FootStepInfo`. `b` is the foot, 0 left and 1 right (2 and 3 in 20,000 phases, likely hands or a quadruped's back feet), and over a phase window that foot moves 16 to 17 cm, so a phase is a planted step. The vector is not a foot position (2.5 to 3 m from every foot and hand) nor the pelvis speed; its length is about 1.

Tag footsteps marks a foot planted while slower than 1 m/s, joins one-frame flickers, counts a swing only if the foot rises 3 cm, and puts 6 or 7 on a swing's highest frame. On 60 of Kliff's clips with foot tags it agrees with the game on 83% of planted frames, against 64% for calling every frame planted, and 57 of the game's 150 step tops fall within 2 frames of its own. The game's tags look placed by hand: the thresholds from 0.4 to 1.4 m/s and 0 to 10 cm all land between 77% and 83%.

## Retargeting

Retarget from armature moves each matched bone by the source bone's turn away from its rest, in world space. The game bone's world rotation is the source bone's world rotation times the inverse of the source rest, times the game bone's rest turned to point as the source bone's rest points. Bones are set parent first each frame, and each child's head comes from its parent, so bone lengths stay the game's. The check rebuilds Kliff's run on a made-up rig and back to within 0.0001 degrees and 0.01 mm.

The first real file, on 4 October, was CMU's motion 141_16, "Wave Hello", from the BVH release at github.com/una-dinosauria/cmu-mocap: 300 frames at 120 a second, which Blender's BVH importer with Scale FPS makes 76 at 30. All 21 of its body joints matched by name; CMU has no third spine bone, so Bip01 Spine2 holds its bind pose. Four things went wrong on the first try, and each is now handled.

The hips' height at rest was 0, because a BVH rest pose has its root at the origin, so the travel was scaled about a million times and Kliff left the picture. Scale now comes from leg length, thigh to knee to ankle, which no pose changes. Height is then set so the feet's lowest joint over the clip sits as low as Kliff's do at rest: where the hip joint sits on the leg differs between rigs, and by leg length alone Kliff floated 15 to 20 cm.

Rest poses differ. CMU's arms are out in a T and Kliff's bind pose has them down at about 65 degrees, so turns taken from each rig's own rest put every arm 65 degrees off. The game bone's rest is now first turned so it points from its joint to the next mapped joint the way the source bone does, the upper arm to the elbow, the thigh to the knee. A bone's own axis says little (the Biped pelvis bone points sideways), so it is joint to joint. A hand or the head has no next joint and turns as its parent does. So does a foot, deliberately: CMU's toe joint sits level with the ankle on a flat foot and Kliff's 9 cm below it, so pointing the foot the same way tipped his toes up by about 24 degrees (his foot slopes 28.5 degrees from ankle to toe, CMU's about 5), while both rest poses stand on flat feet.

CMU's release adds a T-pose as frame 1 of every file, made for retargeting: its neck and head look straight ahead and its arms are 5 degrees below level, while the files' own rest pose splays the legs about 20 degrees and bends the neck forward. Retarget takes a first frame whose two upper arms point level and opposite each other for the source's rest pose and leaves it out of the clip.

The actor faced about 140 degrees away from the T-pose's facing once the motion began, so Kliff waved side-on. Face forward turns the whole motion, rotations and travel, about the vertical by the hips' turn on the first frame.

In game the wave played, but Kliff's legs pressed together. CMU's hip joints sit 43 cm apart at Kliff's size and his 23 cm, and the actor stood with the ankles 31 to 42 cm apart, the legs angled in under those wide hips. The same angles on Kliff's hips put his ankles 14 to 30 cm apart. Retarget now places each ankle where the source's is from its hips, scaled by leg length, swinging the thigh about the hip joint to point there with the knee bend kept, then gives the foot back its own turn. His ankles came out 29 to 37 cm apart; matching direction only, with his own leg reach, leaves them a little closer than the actor's.

The wave also looked fast. CMU lists 141_16 at 120 fps, the rate the file gives, and the forearm reverses every 10 to 13 frames, so the actor waved about 5 times a second; that is the recording.

## The CMU database

## Sounds

The game's bank info, `sound/bin__/windows/soundbanksinfo_<language>.pasound` and the same under `soundcommon/`, names 12,579 and 2,235 Wwise objects. Each record is a u8 length, the name, hashlittle of the name in lower case with seed 0xC5EDE, and the object's Wwise ID, which is FNV-1 of the name, as Wwise makes its IDs. By the banks' HIRC sections 7,452 of the names are events, 6,880 of them starting `sfx_` and 899 `vce_`. The game's own files name sounds in plain text, such as `sfx_move_armor_lp` in `character/descriptors/modelboneanimationscript/soundscript/scripttable.xml`.

No word of a chart's frame events is a Wwise ID. A frame event of type 0x41, 58 words, plays a sound by the hashlittle key at word 48. In Kliff's 96 charts 9,083 of the 16,235 type 0x41 events name a sound of the bank info, and 3,724 set no other word but 52 (-1.0 as a float), 54 (0x10000), 55 (0xFFFF0000) and 57 (0x50000), which is the event a move gets. Type 0x42 plays a voice: word 2 is the key of `voice` and word 7 names the situation, such as `atk_ready`, `dmg_f` or `soc_fear_p`. Type 0x43 names a sound at word 2.

Most banks hold one to nine events, and some are named by the ID of their only event, so the game looks to load a sound's bank when the sound plays. A move's sound goes in `basic_lower` only, as its face does. Sounds lists the 7,452 by reading every bank once per game version, about 7 s, and keeps the list in the add-on's cache.

In game on 4 October Kliff clapped on every round of the Charleston on 8, about four rounds before 8 stopped it, and the other moves' sounds played as well: a drum hit on the looping wave, his horse-call whistle with the moonwalk and a man's laugh with the short wave. Their banks hold one to nine events each.

To play a move's sound in Blender, the add-on follows its event through the bank the event is in, bank version 150 in all 3,288 banks. An event's record is a count, one byte for fewer than 128, then its action IDs. All three test sounds have one action of type 0x0403, Play, whose target ID follows the type. Each target is a random container, type 5, holding 3 to 7 variants. A container's list of children sits behind variable-length parameters, but every sound and container names its parent near the start of its own record. A sound's record starts with 14 bytes of source, its plug-in, stream type, media ID, in-memory size and bits, and a source plug-in adds a size and its parameters. An effects count, a metadata count and the bus come next, and then the parent. Read that way, 89,894 of 89,949 parents are objects in the banks; the rest are records laid out otherwise and are skipped. The preview plays the container's first child by ID, where the game picks one as it plays.

A sound's plug-in names its codec. 57,876 sounds are Wwise Vorbis, 0x00040001, which `formats/wwise.py` already decodes for voice lines; 3,297 are ADPCM, 0x00020001, and the rest plug-ins that make sound as the game runs. Stream type 0 keeps the media in the bank's DATA, indexed by DIDX. Type 1, prefetched, keeps only the start there, so the whole media comes from its file; reading the bank's copy ended the decode early. Type 2 is in its file only, `sound/windows/media/<id>.wem` or `soundcommon/windows/media/<id>.wem`, and voices under the language's folder there. A voice's event is in three banks, one per installed language, `sound/windows/korean/`, `english(us)/` and `chinese(prc)/`, each naming its own language's media, so the event list keeps the English bank. Keeping the first found, the Korean, left 170 voices, Kliff's attack grunts among them, pointing at media only the Korean folder holds. Of the 7,452 sounds a move can play, 7,095 decode to Ogg this way; 215 are not Vorbis, 121 are music, 20 play no sound of their own and one, `vce_effort_kliff`, names media the game does not have.

The weapon bone's offset, the socket and the cup scale with the body, while the festival character's fingers come out within 1 mm of Kliff's, so on the fourth try the festival's own weapon bone left the cup an inch or two out of his fist. For a cup the add-on now keys the festival's fingers and weapon bone turn, with the bone's location solved in Blender, both characters built at their own proportions, so that his fist sits where the festival character's sits on the cup, 2.7 cm nearer his palm. A cup in his left hand is refused. The game never holds one there, and the festival's pose mirrored onto his left hand left the cup 13 cm off his palm, as the grip is off to one side. On 4 October 2026 he held cup_0006 right on 8. The cup lies on its side while his arm hangs, as a mug held by its handle does.

A move's events go on its node last first, each before the node's others at its time, so a chart made again keeps their order. Before, two events at the same time swapped places on every rebuild.

## Gear

The game's data tables are in group 0008 as pairs of files, `gamedata/binarystaticinfo__/bin/<table>.staticinfoheader` and `.staticinfobody`. The header is a u16 record count, then each record's u32 key and u32 offset into the body; a record runs to the next offset. Each record starts with its key again and its name, a u32 length and the bytes. `formats/staticinfo.py` reads them and finds the fields it needs by their shape, checking each against the game's files, without decoding the rest.

characterinfo has 7,250 records. Every one names four files side by side as `hashlittle(name, 0xC5EDE)`: the character description's base name, the appearance's full path, an empty name (0xEAC5E173) and the skeleton's full path. Kliff, key 1, names `phm_description_player_kliff`, `character/appearance/1_pc/1_phm/cd_phm_macduff/cd_phm_macduff_00000.app_xml` and `character/model/1_pc/1_phm/phm_01.pab`. Kliff_Clone, Kliff_AI and four more records name the same appearance; the first in the table is taken. The 7,250 records name 4,478 appearances.

A record's items are lists of a u32 count and that many 64-byte entries, each a u32 iteminfo key and seven numbers the add-on does not use. A record holds several lists, its loot among them, and a list of small keys can turn up by chance, since Money_Copper is key 1. The gear is the first list with an item modelled for the character's family, `cd_phm_` for a man. Kliff's is at byte 590: Wolf_OneHandSword, Wood_OneHandShield, Rusty_Hagwood_OneHandDagger, GreyWolf_OneHandBow, Inytium_Leather_Armor, Inytium_Leather_Cloak, Inytium_Leather_Gloves, Inytium_Leather_Boots, Item_Fist_Kliff and Lantern. The family test keeps a greyhound whose record lists a priest's shield from carrying it.

iteminfo has 6,816 records. An item's models are sets, each three floats of scale, 1 1 1 so far, a u32 count and that many prefab name hashes, `hashlittle(lower-case prefab name, 0xC5EDE)`. The first set is the item worn or held: the sword's is `cd_phm_01_sword_0019_r` and its scabbard `cd_phm_01_sword_0053_r_in`. Later sets are the left-hand version and the model dropped on the ground (`cd_t0254_dropitem_*`). Item_Fist_Kliff's model is `cd_t9999_empty`, which the add-on skips.

The armour items are the wearable versions. An NPC's appearance has its own version of the same armour, `cd_m0001_00_no_phm_ub_43005` where the northern warrior's record gives `cd_phm_m0001_00_no_phm_ub_43005`, and the two differ in parts and materials. So only a playable character, one under `1_pc`, takes its armour from its gear; anyone else keeps its appearance and gets the items with socket rules, unless its appearance already has a part of the same component, as the spear guards do.

## Smaller points

- In a LOD clip only the 8-bit blocks count 1/5 second ticks. Its half float blocks, which hold `Bip01`, `B_MoveControl_01` and the TL bones, keep 30 fps frame numbers. In the LOD run clip `B_MoveControl_01` ends on 48 while the 8-bit blocks end on tick 8. Version 0.1.0 scaled both and played LOD root motion six times too slowly.
- A LOD clip's last tick rounds up, so it can sit past the clip's last frame: 34 frames end on tick 6 (frame 36), 75 frames on tick 13.
- Four leaf bones in `phm_01.pab` have a bind scale of 1.0014 or 1.053, the eyeballs and `Bip01 L/R Elbow_In`. Blender bones hold no rest scale, so on a leaf bone it is dropped and changes nothing visible. On 18 skeletons a bone with children has one. The horse's tail runs 1.85, 1.14, 1.17 and 1.16. The importer places each bone through the scaled hierarchy, which `tests/check_skeletons.py` checks on all 273 skeletons to 0.008 mm, but a key that moves such a child is not scaled the way the game would scale it.
- Every bone name of Kliff, the broom, the Wyvern, the horse and the bear resolves from the clips' hashes.
- Whether a scale key replaces the bind scale or multiplies it is not settled. The importer treats it like rotation and position, on top of the bind pose.

## Every layout the game ships

On 3 October 2026 the codec in 0.6.0 read 68,846 of the 280,011 full clips in group 0009, and the skeleton reader 172 of 273 skeletons. A survey of every file sorted the failures by header, and four findings cover all but three clips. `formats/paa_codec.py` has the full layout.

The u16 at 0x10 is a set of flags that say which lead fields are present. 0x01 and 0x04 each add a transform record of ten floats, scale, rotation and position. 0x02 adds `ref_scale`, 0x40 the skeleton's path. The duration and the block counts always follow. The broom clips have 0x0F, so the old reader's fixed lead fit them. A Kliff clip with flags 0x02 starts with `ref_scale` right after the header, and one with 0x00 with its duration.

The block field earlier versions called flags, 0 in every broom clip, is a count of scale keys. Each key is a frame and three half floats, read before the rotation keys, and the 8-bit LOD blocks hold them as three bytes in 1/32 steps, where 32 is 1.0. The bear idle's `B_MoveControl_01` has one, which is what broke it before. About 79,000 tracks in a 20,000-clip sample have scale keys. Almost all of the body tracks hold a constant 1.0, some hold a bind scale such as 1.053, and facial clips animate scale on each axis.

Flag 0x100 replaces the two block counts with a u16 5 and five counts, one per key kind. Kind 1 is the 8-bit LOD block, kind 2 half floats throughout and kind 3 half float rotations with float positions. Kinds 0 and 4 are counted and never used. All 151,000 autofacial clips, Kliff's Wyvern riding clips and every LOD use this. The LOD layout the codec knew, a u32 5, two counts and a u32 0, is the same thing read with different field boundaries.

Bit 0x10 adds a u32 after the header, the hash of the full clip, and marks a LOD. A LOD's flags are its full clip's with 0x190 added, in all 3,000 pairs checked, so the exporter derives them. The u16 at 0x12 is 0xC000 for a UTF-8 tag, 0x8000 for a UTF-16 tag counted in characters with no terminator, or 0. Flags 0x08, 0x20 and 0x80 change nothing in the layout; what they mean is not known, and the codec keeps them.

With these, every full clip but three and all 267,132 LOD clips decode with every byte and key count accounted for and encode back byte for byte. The three are `cd_m0002_rabbit_basic_00_00_abn_capture_000`, `..._abn_die_000` and `cd_phm_longsword_01_01_att_skill_normal_combo_c_004`, with flags 0x30 and 0x70, where bit 0x10 does not fit the LOD reading.

Skeletons put exactly 2 bytes between bone records in all 273 files. After the last bone comes a trailer of per-bone tables and matrices that the add-on keeps without reading. The old reader tried gaps of 2, 26, 38, 58 and 62, taken from a reader for an older game by the same studio, and on 101 skeletons one of those landed inside the trailer and read it as bones. The reader now takes 2 and stops when the next record has no printable name, no earlier parent or no unit quaternion.
